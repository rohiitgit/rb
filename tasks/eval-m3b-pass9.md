## New findings

### L1: major. A member only javac sees is never checked. A call inside it becomes an exact edge from the enclosing class.

**Cause.** `structure_disagreement` (`crates/rb-indexer/src/scip.rs:668`) builds clause 2's ranges only from definitions anchored to a Tree-sitter definition:
- `anchored` at `:679`;
- the filter at `:686`.

A method, constructor or type that javac compiles but Tree-sitter reads as comment text is therefore never anchored. Its enclosing range is passed over as if it were a field or lambda, which amendment 68 says "both sides credit to the enclosing definition". This is not such a case: Tree-sitter puts the call in an initializer block or a field initializer of the class. The innermost anchored range is the class, which is the Tree-sitter source, so both clauses hold.

`definition_disagreement` (`:865`) does not catch it either: the javac-only definition occurrence sits inside a Tree-sitter comment at class level, not on a token and not inside a Tree-sitter method.

**Repro.** Real image, `--no-cache --force`, project `t9esc` (UTF-8 pom, ASCII sources). javac compiles every file, and `rb status` reads `documents rejected 4 (structure_disagreement 4)`: Esc1–3 and Hid8 only.

### L2: minor. `encoding_ambiguous` accepts a tie. In an ISO-8859-1 build, `<init>` at `this(…)` lands on a later token, and the true edge is lost without a count.

**Cause.** `encoding_ambiguous` (`crates/rb-scip/src/decode.rs:327`, rule at `:364`: `iso > utf8`) rejects a document only when the single-byte reading names *more* tokens. javac's `<init>` occurrence at `this` (or `super`) never names its own token. Under the UTF-8 reading it can land on a same-named token later on the line and count for UTF-8. That offsets the one genuine ISO-8859-1 occurrence: 1:1, accepted.

**Repro.** Real image, `t9lat`: UTF-8 pom, `.mvn/maven.config` `-Dproject.build.sourceEncoding=ISO-8859-1`, javac read ISO-8859-1.
- **`Wxyz.java` line 6:** `  Wxyz() { /* ` + 9 × `é` + ` */ this(1); Wxyz(); }`, with `Wxyz(int q)` and `int Wxyz()` declared above.
  - Raw occurrences: `` Wxyz#`<init>`(). `` at `[5,36,40]` (the `this`); `Wxyz#Wxyz().` at `[5,45,49]`.
  - Decoded: `` `<init>`() `` at `[5,45,5,49]`, which is the method call's `Wxyz`; `Wxyz#Wxyz()` at `[5,54,5,54]`.
  - Exact edge: `` Wxyz#`<init>`(+1). CALLS Wxyz#`<init>`(). `` at `[5,45,5,49]`.
- **`Abcd.java`:** the same with 13 × `é` and `new Abcd()`. Exact `` Abcd#`<init>`(+1). REFERENCES Abcd#`<init>`(). `` at the `Abcd` of `new Abcd()`, whose true symbol is `` `<init>`(+1) ``.
- **Status line:** `documents rejected 0`, `unmatched 0`.

**Observed vs expected.**
- The evidence span names a call of `Wxyz#Wxyz()` and claims a call of the `(int)` constructor.
- The true `Wxyz() → Wxyz#Wxyz()` is lost and counted nowhere.
- The relation itself (`Wxyz()` invokes `Wxyz(int)` through `this(1)`, on the same line) is true. That is why this is minor and not major. It is still an invariant-6 violation.
- Amendment 71 says a single-byte encoding declared "anywhere else" is covered by `encoding_ambiguous`. On a tie it is not.

**Suggested fix.** Reject on a tie when the single-byte reading names at least one token (`iso > 0 && iso >= utf8`). Or leave occurrences whose truth form `may_name` cannot read (`<init>` at `this`/`super`, zero-width) out of the UTF-8 count.

**Regression test.** A decoder unit with the measured `Wxyz` raw ranges, expecting `EncodingAmbiguous`. Mutant E3 below (tie rejected) currently survives.
